## What the Archive Is

Your archive is two things working together:

**The audio** — digitized recordings from your tapes, stored safely in the cloud. Private. Yours. Not shared publicly, not streamed, just preserved.

**The catalogue** — a public-facing website where you document each tape. The station. The year. The state you were in. And most importantly — what you remember about it. That part is entirely yours and nobody else can write it.

Visitors to your site will be able to read your catalogue and hear a 30-second sample from each recovered tape. They won't get the full recording. They'll get your memory of it, your notes about it, and a taste of what you saved.

That's the archive. Simple, honest, and completely yours.

## Your Storage — Google One

Your digitized FLAC files (the full archival recordings) will live in Google Drive under your archive account.

**The plan:** Google One 2TB — approximately $10 per month.

At roughly 450MB per tape, your estimated collection of 2,500 tapes would use around 1.1TB if every tape were recoverable. Since some of the older tapes — especially from the early 1980s — may not play back after forty years of storage, the actual storage you'll use is likely well under that. 2TB gives you comfortable room to grow without worrying about running out of space.

**What lives in Google Drive:**
- Your full FLAC recordings (archival masters — the complete digitized tape)
- Organized in folders by decade and state

**What lives on your WordPress site:**
- Your 30-second MP3 clips (one per tape entry)
- Your catalogue posts
- Your words and memories

The full recordings stay private in your Drive. The catalogue and samples are what the world sees.

## Your Digitization Workflow

For each tape you're ready to digitize:

**Step 1 — Assess the tape**
Look at it. Does it look okay? Rewind it gently. Play the first ten seconds. Does it sound right? If something seems wrong, set it aside and note it as needing assessment. Don't force it.

**Step 2 — Record in Audition**
Play the tape on the correct recorder. Audition captures the audio through the Behringer interface. Let it run.

**Step 3 — Export your FLAC master**
Export the full recording as a FLAC file. This is your archival master — the complete, lossless recording. Save it to Google Drive in the correct decade and state folder.

**Step 4 — Choose your 30 seconds**
Listen back and find the 30 seconds you want to represent this tape on your site. It can be a song, a DJ you remember, a news break, a moment of static clearing into signal — whatever feels right to you. You choose. Export that clip as an MP3.

**Step 5 — Write your catalogue entry**
Open your WordPress site and fill in the tape entry template. The most important field is yours — what do you remember about this tape?

## When a Tape Won't Play

Some tapes — especially the oldest ones — may not play back. The tape itself may have degraded over forty years. This is not a failure of your project. It is honest archiving.

When a tape won't play:

- You still write the catalogue entry
- You note the condition and what happened when you tried to play it
- You write whatever you remember about it — the station, the year, what you think was on it
- You mark it as Unrecoverable
- The 30-second sample field gets a written description instead of audio

**Your memory of that tape is the only surviving record that it ever existed and was heard by someone. That belongs in your archive too.**

Real archives have damaged and missing items. Yours will too. That's not a gap — that's history.

## Your Catalogue Entry Template

Every tape gets its own page on your site. Here is what you fill in for each one:

***

**Tape ID**
We assign these in order as you catalogue them. T-0001, T-0002, T-0003, and so on. You never have to think about this one.

**Recorded Approximately**
The year, or your best guess at a year range. Month if you know it.
*Example: Spring 1989 / Around 1993-1994 / Late 80s*

**Station / Source**
The call letters if you know them. The frequency. Or just your best description.
*Example: KBCO Boulder / KOA 850 AM / Don't remember — classic rock station, Colorado*

**Geography**
The city and state where you were when you recorded this.
*Example: Denver, Colorado / Somewhere in Kansas, road trip*

**Recorder Used**

[ ] Not sure

**Format**

[ ] Reel-to-Reel

**Tape Brand** *(if you can still read the label)*
*Example: TDK D90 / Maxell XLII / Generic / Label gone*

**What's On It**
[ ] Music
[ ] Talk / DJ
[ ] News
[ ] Sports
[ ] Mixed
[ ] Not sure

**Playback**
[ ] Recovered — plays fully
[ ] Recovered — partial, some dropout or damage
[ ] Unrecoverable — could not play back
[ ] Not yet digitized

**Condition Notes**
A sentence or two about the physical condition of the tape and what happened when you tried to play it. Only what you actually observed.
*Example: Shell is cracked on one corner but tape runs fine. Slight dropout around the 20 minute mark.*
*Example: Tape wouldn't move. Appears stuck. Set aside.*

### What You Remember

*This is the most important field. Write whatever comes to mind. There is no right length, no right format, no rules. Just what you remember.*

*Where were you? Why did you record this? What was happening in your life? Was there something specific on this tape you wanted to keep? Do you remember listening back to it? Anything at all.*

The tape is still in the original case with my handwriting on the label — I can barely read it now but I know it says Sunday."*

*Your words. Your memory. Nobody else can write this part.*
